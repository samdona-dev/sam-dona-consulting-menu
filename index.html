Sam Dona introduces — a practical menu of six services — built by a person who controls the AI, not the other way around — that turn AI into a business enabler.

01

AI needs diagnostic

Before anything gets built, we sit down with the key people in the business to find out what's actually needed — so spend goes toward a real problem, not a trend.

In practiceAn owner says "I want AI in the business" but isn't sure why. We run a microscope-and-telescope review — close up on the day-to-day friction, then zoomed out on the bigger picture — to find the real issue: rising costs, cashflow gaps, slow invoicing, missed follow-ups, or manual reporting. Then we recommend only what solves that.
02

AI governance & policy

Set the rules AI has to operate within — what's allowed, what isn't, and who signs off before it touches something important.

In practiceStaff are already using tools like ChatGPT, Claude, and Copilot day to day, but nobody's said what's off-limits. We write the rulebook: private client data pasted in, contracts drafted unsupervised — either one can breach customer privacy, void your professional indemnity cover, or leave a gap in your public liability protection if something goes wrong. We put boundaries in place before that happens, not after.
03

Custom AI algorithm development

Off-the-shelf AI tools don't know how your business actually runs. We build the logic around your real workflows instead.

In practiceA construction company wanted a live client portal for every project — build stages, progress payments, variations needing approval, warranty periods, and the Occupation Certificate paperwork a private certifier needs before sign-off — all tracked in one place instead of chased across emails and folders. Generic software doesn't know their cost codes, stage sequencing, or trade structure, so the logic gets built from scratch, trained on how the business really operates. Updates run day-to-day by someone with no technical background — no code, no developer on standby.
04

AI risk & safety rails

A checkpoint that catches AI mistakes before they become real losses — for the owner and for everyone using the tool.

In practiceAn AI tool suggests a supplier payment amount. Instead of it going straight out the door, a safety rail forces a human to check anything over a set threshold before it's approved.
05

Enterprise systems alignment

Making sure new AI work actually talks to the systems you already run, so it modernizes the business instead of disrupting it.

In practiceA business runs Xero and wants to add an AI reporting tool. Without alignment, it pulls messy or duplicate data and the reports come out wrong. We make sure the AI connects to Xero properly — clean data in, reliable numbers out.
06

Executive coaching

The systems can be sound and the policy watertight, but the owner still has to make the calls. This is one-on-one support building that judgment and confidence.

In practiceA principal is being asked by staff and clients about AI almost weekly and doesn't feel equipped to answer. Regular sessions build their own fluency and decision-making, so they're leading the change, not reacting to it.

Where this comes from

This approach was built over 30+ years inside large organisations, long before "AI governance" was a phrase anyone used — reconciling systems, policy, and finance controls at scale. That same discipline is now applied to AI, as a strategic enabler rather than a brake on the business.
